# 0. Outline: the shape of the proof, as far as we have read it

*Written by Kepler, 2026-11-22, from notes 1 to 6; read whole by Halley before it was published; Halley's six corrections are folded in. Every statement below was re-read in the release's source by the writer on the day of writing. The page says what the Lean statements say. It does not say why the hard steps are true: no proof below the level shown here has been read by us, and nothing here has been checked by a mathematician.*

## Read this first

The release proves the Riemann Hypothesis in Mathlib's formulation, and four companion statements about Dirichlet L-functions. Reading only *statements* (theorem signatures and definitions), top down, we find the following shape:

1. Everything rests on one statement: **no Dirichlet L-function vanishes for Re s > 1/2** (apart from the pole of ζ).
2. That statement is obtained from an analytic criterion: a weighted sum over primes, attached to a primitive character ψ, is bounded by x^(1/2 + O(ε)) on certain long *windows* of x, and a second theorem turns such a bound on windows into a zero-free half-plane. (Our reading: the criterion looks classical, and the second theorem is of Landau type; the library's own folder name is `LandauTheorems`.)
3. The windows, and the bound on them, come from objects the library calls *families*. Their existence is derived from **three inputs**: a statement on characters of GL_d over finite fields (our reading: J. A. Green's theorem of 1955), a bound on Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists over subfields of cyclotomic fields, and a structure of 46 conditions that lives in the library's theta-correspondence files.

*Our judgement, not a finding:* steps 1 and 2 are short and a number theorist can check them quickly from the pointers below; whatever is new must lie under step 3, where we have read only the statements of the inputs.

## The chain

Paths are relative to the release's `lean/src/`. Each row names the Lean declaration that performs the step and the note that reads it.

```
RiemannHypothesis                                             (Mathlib's definition)
  ⇑  functional equation of ζ                                 step 1
UpperTheory.EveryCharacter      L(χ, s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1/2, all χ
  ⇑  primitive → all characters                               step 2
Formalism.HC.ZeroFreeAll        the same, for primitive χ
  ⇑  window bound + Landau step + families at scale la         step 3
for every primitive ψ, at the level Level.cyc ψ:
    (A) tied EFamC families satisfy  Gm(x) ≤ x^(1/2 + (eD+eL+7)ε)  on their windows
    (B) FormB: a bound Gm(x) ≤ x^θ on windows [Y, Y^la] ⇒ no zero right of θ
    (C) tied EFamC families with windows at scale la exist
  ⇑                                                           step 4
(C) comes from  TopG  and  HostExW (3/2) (GChi ψ).req
  ⇑                                                           step 5
TopG ⇐ Green + HypsAlt (46 fields)          HostExW (3/2) … ⇐ VWinAt ψ 401
```

### Step 1. From `EveryCharacter` to the Riemann Hypothesis ([note 1](01-the-top-of-the-proof.md), [note 2](02-from-everycharacter-to-rh.md))

- `Main.lean` states five theorems; each is one term applied to `allChi : UpperTheory.EveryCharacter` (`Supplements/Kernels/MainTheorems.lean`).
- `Formalism.VW.rh_of_every` (`Formalism/GammaFactorNonvanishing/CritLine.lean`) uses `EveryCharacter` for the character modulo 1 only, whose L-function is ζ. `Formalism.VW.rh_of_right` (`Formalism/GammaFactorNonvanishing/RhOfRight.lean`, which imports only Mathlib) then reflects a zero with Re s < 1/2 across the line by Mathlib's functional equation `riemannZeta_one_sub`.
- *Status:* textbook. Note 2 walks through it line by line and finds two further derivations in the library that `Main.lean` does not use.

### Step 2. From primitive characters to all characters ([note 5](05-the-pivot.md))

- `UpperTheory.Top.imprimitive` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/Imprimitive.lean`, line 5): `ZeroFreeAll` implies the body of `EveryCharacter`, word for word.
- `Formalism.HC.ZeroFreeAll` (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean`, line 18): for every primitive Dirichlet character ψ and every s with Re s > 1/2, L(ψ, s) ≠ 0.
- *Status:* the statement is the classical reduction. We read only the first two lines of its proof.

### Step 3. Where the zeros enter ([note 6](06-where-the-zeros-enter.md))

`Formalism.HC.zeroFreeOn_of_tiedWin_cyc` (`…/TotientGaloisClasses/TopWin.lean`, line 90) derives `ZeroFreeAll` from three hypotheses about each primitive ψ. All three concern one real function, `Level.Gm` (`…/ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean`, line 38), for the level `Level.cyc ψ` generated by the powers of ψ:

> Gm(y) = Gz(y) + Σ over the non-trivial primitive characters ψ′ of the level of wt(ψ′) · Re gChi(ψ′, y),
>
> Gz(x) = Σ_{n ≤ x} Λ(n)·(x/n − 1) − x log x + (1 + γ)·x  (`Formalism/StatementOC.lean`, lines 11-13),

where Λ is Mathlib's von Mangoldt function, γ is Euler's constant, `gChi(χ, y)` for χ ≠ 1 is Σ_{n ≤ y} Λ(n)·χ(n)·max(y/n − 1, 0) + y·L′(1, χ)/L(1, χ) (`…/TotientGaloisClasses/Analytic.lean`, lines 11-24), and each weight is (3·o + 1)⁻¹, where o is the number of characters in the group generated by ψ (`Level.ofSub`, same file as `Gm`, line 114). The definition of `wt` (line 36) allows weight 1 for one distinguished character, but the level used here has none (`psK := none`, line 112).

- **(A) The window bound.** `ChebyshevInequalityOnWindows` (`TopWin.lean`, lines 26-28): for every ε > 0 and every family that satisfies `EFamC` for the level and is "tied" on its window set with constants eD, eL, there is x₁ with Gm(x) ≤ x^(1/2 + (eD + eL + 7)·ε) for all x ≥ x₁ in that window set. Proved for every level by `UpperTheory.Comparison₂.level_gm_le_on_family_scales` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/LevelGmLeOnFamilyScales.lean`, line 28).
- **(B) The Landau step.** `Level.FormBAt la` (`…/TotientGaloisClasses/Window.lean`, line 30): for 0 < θ < 1, if for every y there is Y ≥ y with Gm(x) ≤ x^θ on the whole interval [Y, Y^la], then no L-function of the level vanishes for Re s > θ. Proved for every level and every la > 1 by `UpperTheory.Comparison₂.formB` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/Imports.lean`, line 8).
- **(C) Families at the right scale.** `ChebyshevInequalityAtWindowScale la` (`TopWin.lean`, line 30): there are eD, eL and ε₁ > 0 such that for every 0 < ε < ε₁ a family exists that satisfies `EFamC` for the level, is tied on its windows, and has windows at scale la. (`EFamC` is defined in `…/TotientGaloisClasses/TopDefs.lean`, line 15; we have not read past its one line.)

*Interpretation (ours; check it).* (A) is square-root cancellation in a smoothed sum over primes, but only on windows; (B) is a theorem of Landau type, which needs the bound only on a sequence of long intervals; so (C), the supply of windows, carries the weight. The small weights (3·o + 1)⁻¹ look like a positivity device. A number theorist will see at once whether these readings are right.

### Step 4. Where the families come from ([note 5](05-the-pivot.md), [note 6](06-where-the-zeros-enter.md))

- `UpperTheory.Top.zeroFreeAll_of_topG` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ZeroFreeAllOfTopG.lean`, line 26) takes la > 1, `TopG`, and `HostExW la (GChi ψ).req` for every primitive ψ, and concludes `ZeroFreeAll`. Its ten-line proof supplies (A) and (B) by the two theorems above and builds (C) from `TopG`. Note 6 reads it in full.
- `TopG` (same file, line 12): for every scale la > 1 and every requirement G, if `HostExW la G.req` holds and every family in the class `G.cls la` satisfies `HypV`, then that class is `FamilyClassConnected`.
- The path uses la = 3/2 (`…/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/TopPlug.lean`, line 14).
- *Not read:* what `HostExW`, `GChi`, `HypV`, `FamilyClassConnected`, a "family" and "tied" (`ChainTied`, an existential over 22 objects with 16 conditions) mean. These definitions are the next thing to read.

## Addition, 2026-11-22 (later the same day): what reading the proofs added

*Notes 7 to 12 were written after the page above; this section was read whole by Halley before it was published. This section says, in the order of the chain, what they add and what they leave open. Each line rests on the note it cites, where the anchors are; the declarations named here were opened by the writer of this section the same day. The page above is unchanged.*

**The proof in three movements** ([note 11](11-hosts-requirements-families.md)). The words the page above could not explain ("families", "hosts", "connected") now have a reading:

1. *Find good hosts.* A host (`Formalism.HC.HostRaw`) is a Galois number field inside the cyclotomic field of N = QS·q·d, containing a square root of −d, built from two auxiliary primes q and d. For each primitive character ψ, `UpperTheory.Top.supplyChi` turns the Tate–Shafarevich bound into "good hosts meeting ψ's requirement exist at every scale of a system of windows" (`HostExW`). The requirement (`GChi ψ`) asks for two things: conditions on auxiliary characters, and a Tate–Shafarevich bound for the host's field. The proof that supplies hosts shows the host's field is one of the fields the bound speaks of.
2. *Turn hosts into tied families.* `TopG`: from hosts whose fields satisfy that bound, families exist that are "tied" on their windows. "Connected" (`FamilyClassConnected`) means only that; nothing topological. This is the step that Green's theorem and the 46-field structure supply, and it is the part we have read least.
3. *Cash it in.* A tied family gives the window bound, and the Landau step turns the window bound into no zeros to the right of 1/2.

**How much stands behind each step** ([note 7](07-map-of-the-library.md)). By import closure, an upper bound: the Landau step needs at most 30 of the release's 175,187 files, the whole analytic criterion at most 754, the step joining the three inputs at most 17,276, and 157,604 files stand behind the file that derives the Tate–Shafarevich bound.

**The Landau step, read as a proof** ([note 8](08-the-landau-step.md)). All 31 files were opened and their proofs read, with two long routine computations skimmed. The argument rests on one inequality for the Taylor coefficients a_k of a Laplace transform of the prime sum: |a_k(σ + iγ)| + Re a_k(σ) is at most twice the integral of the positive part of the sum against the kernel u^k e^(−σu)/k!. The classical form is Montgomery–Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I*, §15.1, which needs the bound for all large x; the library's form needs it only on windows, because it fixes σ and lets k grow. *Our identification; we did not find the windowed form in the source we opened.* The smoothing weight (y/n − 1) makes the residue at a real zero positive, which is what lets an upper bound exclude real zeros too (our reading, note 8).

**The window bound, and why it is not circular** ([note 9](09-the-window-bound.md)). `UpperTheory.Comparison₂.core_gm` proves Gm(x) ≤ x^(1/2 + O(ε)) by estimating one average over a finite group from both sides. It uses square-root bounds for auxiliary characters, the twists of powers of ψ by the Jacobi symbols of q and d. Those are not assumed for all x: they are proved on a finite range only, for a good pair (q, d) chosen at each scale. *That this makes the argument non-circular is our reading.* The other side of the estimate is an upper bound, with the correct main term, for a weighted count of inert primes (`ASK ≤ Main + error`); that sharp one-sided bound is what the tied families must deliver.

**How the good pair is found** ([note 10](10-how-the-good-pair-is-found.md), [note 12](12-the-zero-density-box.md)). A mean-square bound over the primes q, obtained by opening a square and using quadratic reciprocity, shows that most q are good, then most d. Two classical inputs are proved inside the library: Siegel's theorem (`siegelLower`; 46 files in its import closure) and a zero-density estimate (`zero_density_box`: at most C·Q^(1/4) primitive characters of modulus at most Q have a zero with Re s > 1 − 1/400 and |Im s| ≤ Q; 26 files in its import closure). One finding a number theorist may want to check: on the call sites a name search found and we read, the bound derived from Siegel's theorem is used only at the strengths ε = 1 and ε = 1/2, and each use would be served, by our arithmetic, by the effective bound of Montgomery–Vaughan's Corollary 11.12. So the argument, as far as we have read it, does not need Siegel's non-effective strength, although the proof as written invokes it.

**New questions for a mathematician,** in addition to the four above:

5. Is the windowed form of Landau's theorem in note 8 in the literature?
6. Is the two-sided estimate of note 9 a known device, and is "a sharp upper bound for a weighted count of inert primes" a recognisable target?
7. Is our reading of the effectivity of the exceptional-zero inputs (note 10's clarification) right?
8. What should `TopG` mean to a specialist? Note 11 conjectures that small Tate–Shafarevich groups of the host's field force a weighted count of inert primes to be at most its main term, up to an error term. Is that the right reading?

**Still not read:** what "tied" asks in detail (`ChainTied`, and the eleven components of `SizeData₂`); the proof of `TopG` from Green's theorem and the 46 fields; the proof of the Tate–Shafarevich bound itself; and the theorems that fill the 46 fields. Those are the next layers.
